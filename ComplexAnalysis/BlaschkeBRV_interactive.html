Uniformly Bounded Radial Variation for the $a_k = 1 - 1/k^2$ Blaschke Product
and Resolution of a Problem on $T_g$ Acting on $H^\infty$

Austin Anderson
Interactive 3-Level Edition — Grounded in Machine-Checked Lean 4 Proofs (BlaschkeBRV.lean)
November 5, 2026

Abstract. Let $T_g f(z) = \int_0^z f(w) g'(w)\,dw$ and $S_g f(z) = \int_0^z f'(w) g(w)\,dw$ denote the generalized Volterra operator and its companion acting on $H^\infty(\mathbb{D})$. In [Anderson, Jovovic, and Smith, Integral Equations Operator Theory 80 (2014), Problem 4.3], the authors asked for a zero-sequence characterization of Blaschke products belonging to the class $\mathrm{BRV} = \{g \in H(\mathbb{D}) : \sup_{\theta} \int_0^1 |g'(te^{i\theta})|\,dt < \infty\} \subseteq T[H^\infty] = S[H^\infty]$, singling out the Blaschke product $B(z)$ with zeros $a_k = 1 - 1/k^2$ as a critical borderline test case whose individual radii have rectifiable images, but for which a uniform arc-length bound was unknown. We resolve Problem 4.3 in the affirmative by proving that for $a_k = 1 - 1/k^2$—and more generally for $a_k = 1 - 1/k^p$ for every $p > 1$—the Blaschke product $B(z)$ belongs to $\mathrm{BRV}$, and its near-boundary radial variation actually vanishes as $\theta \to 0^+$. Consequently, $T_B$ and $S_B$ are bounded (and non-compact) linear operators on $H^\infty(\mathbb{D})$.

1.Introduction and Operator-Theoretic Background

Let $\mathbb{D} = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| < 1\}$ be the open unit disk, $H(\mathbb{D})$ the space of holomorphic functions on $\mathbb{D}$, $H^\infty = H^\infty(\mathbb{D})$ the Banach algebra of bounded holomorphic functions with norm $\|f\|_\infty = \sup_{z \in \mathbb{D}} |f(z)|$, and $A = A(\mathbb{D})$ the disk algebra of functions in $H^\infty$ extending continuously to $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$. For a symbol $g \in H(\mathbb{D})$, the Volterra-type integral operator $T_g$, its companion operator $S_g$, and the multiplication operator $M_g$ are defined for $f \in H(\mathbb{D})$ and $z \in \mathbb{D}$ by \begin{equation}\label{eq:operators} T_g f(z) = \int_0^z f(w) g'(w)\,dw, \qquad S_g f(z) = \int_0^z f'(w) g(w)\,dw, \qquad M_g f(z) = f(z) g(z). \end{equation} Integration by parts connects the three operators via $M_g f(z) = f(0) g(0) + T_g f(z) + S_g f(z)$. Writing $L[X] = \{g \in H(\mathbb{D}) : L_g \text{ is bounded on } X\}$ and $L_0[X] = \{g \in H(\mathbb{D}) : L_g \text{ is compact on } X\}$, Proposition 2.3 of [AJS14] shows that on $H^\infty$, \begin{equation}\label{eq:TH-SH} T[H^\infty] = S[H^\infty] \subsetneq M[H^\infty] = H^\infty. \end{equation}

For $\theta \in [-\pi, \pi]$, the radial variation of $g \in H(\mathbb{D})$ along the radius $e^{i\theta}$ is $V(g, \theta) := \int_0^1 |g'(t e^{i\theta})|\,dt$, and the class of analytic functions with uniformly bounded radial variation is \begin{equation}\label{eq:BRV-def} \mathrm{BRV} := \Bigl\{ g \in H(\mathbb{D}) : \|g\|_{\mathrm{BRV}} := \sup_{\theta \in [-\pi, \pi]} \int_0^1 |g'(t e^{i\theta})|\,dt < \infty \Bigr\}. \end{equation} Since $|T_g f(r e^{i\theta})| = \bigl|\int_0^r f(t e^{i\theta}) g'(t e^{i\theta}) e^{i\theta}\,dt\bigr| \le V(g, \theta) \|f\|_\infty$, one has $\mathrm{BRV} \subseteq T[H^\infty] = S[H^\infty]$ with $\|T_g\|_{H^\infty \to H^\infty} \le \|g\|_{\mathrm{BRV}}$.

1.1.Blaschke Products in $\mathrm{BRV}$ and Problem 4.3 of [AJS14]

Prior to the present work, two contrasting regimes of Blaschke products $B(z) = \prod_k \frac{|a_k|}{a_k} \frac{a_k - z}{1 - \bar{a}_k z}$ were understood in [AJS14]:

  1. Uniformly Frostman Blaschke Products ($B \in \mathrm{BRV}$): By Cargo's theorem [Car61], every subproduct of $B$ belongs to $\mathrm{BRV}$ if and only if $\sup_{\theta} \sum_k \frac{1 - |a_k|}{|a_k - e^{i\theta}|} < \infty$. However, no infinite sequence $\{a_k\}$ approaching $\partial\mathbb{D}$ nontangentially (and in particular no real zero sequence $0 < a_k < 1$) can satisfy the uniform Frostman condition.
  2. Real Interpolating Blaschke Products ($B \notin T[H^\infty]$): By [AJS14, Proposition 2.7 and Theorem 2.10], if $0 < a_k < a_{k+1} < 1$ is an interpolating sequence ($\inf_k \prod_{j \ne k} \bigl|\frac{a_j - a_k}{1 - a_j a_k}\bigr| > 0$), then $B \notin \mathrm{BRV}$ and $T_B$ is unbounded on $H^\infty$.
Problem 1.1 ([AJS14, Problem 4.3, p. 14]). Characterize, in terms of their zero sequences, Blaschke products in $\mathrm{BRV}$. A possible candidate for a Blaschke product in $\mathrm{BRV}$ is the Blaschke product with zeros $\{1 - 1/k^2\}$. It is not hard to see that the image of every radius is rectifiable, but it is not clear that there is a uniform bound for their arc-lengths.

1.2.Main Results

We resolve Problem 1.1 in the affirmative for the entire polynomial-clustering family $a_k = 1 - k^{-p}$ ($p > 1$).

Theorem 1.2 (Main Theorem: Resolution of Problem 4.3 of [AJS14]). For every $p > 1$, let $a_1 = 0$ and $a_k = 1 - 1/k^p \in (0, 1)$ for $k \ge 2$, and let \begin{equation}\label{eq:Bp-def} B(z) = -z \prod_{k=2}^\infty \frac{a_k - z}{1 - a_k z}, \qquad z \in \mathbb{D}, \end{equation} be the corresponding Blaschke product (in particular, $p = 2$ gives $a_k = 1 - 1/k^2$). Then:
  1. $B$ is not uniformly Frostman, and $\{a_k\}$ is not an interpolating sequence ($\rho(a_k, a_{k+1}) \sim \frac{p}{2k} \to 0$).
  2. $B$ has uniformly bounded radial variation on $\mathbb{D}$: \begin{equation}\label{eq:main-BRV} \|B\|_{\mathrm{BRV}} = \sup_{\theta \in [-\pi, \pi]} \int_0^1 |B'(t e^{i\theta})|\,dt < \infty, \end{equation} and hence $B \in \mathrm{BRV} \subseteq T[H^\infty] = S[H^\infty]$. Moreover, the near-boundary radial variation vanishes as $\theta \to 0^+$.
  3. Both $T_B$ and $S_B$ are bounded linear operators on $H^\infty(\mathbb{D})$, whereas $B \notin A(\mathbb{D})$ and therefore $T_B$ is bounded but not compact on $H^\infty(\mathbb{D})$.

2.Borderline Geometry and Pseudohyperbolic Clustering of $a_k = 1 - 1/k^2$

By the Leibniz rule $(g_1 g_2)' = g_1' g_2 + g_1 g_2'$, any two functions $g_1, g_2 \in \mathrm{BRV} \cap H^\infty$ satisfy $V(g_1 g_2, \theta) \le \|g_2\|_\infty V(g_1, \theta) + \|g_1\|_\infty V(g_2, \theta)$. Since the single factor $b_1(z) = -z$ at $a_1 = 0$ has $\|b_1\|_\infty = 1$ and $V(b_1, \theta) = 1$, removing $b_1$ changes $V(B, \theta)$ by at most $1$. For $k \ge 2$, the zeros $a_k = 1 - k^{-p}$ satisfy $a_k \in [1 - 2^{-p}, 1) \subset [1/2, 1)$.

Proposition 2.1 (Non-Frostman Property and Pseudohyperbolic Collapse). Let $a_k = 1 - 1/k^2$ for $k \ge 2$. Then at $\theta = 0$, every term of the Frostman sum equals $\frac{1 - a_k}{|1 - a_k|} = 1$, so $\sum_{k=2}^{N+1} \frac{1 - a_k}{|1 - a_k|} = N \to \infty$. Moreover, for every $k \ge 2$, the pseudohyperbolic distance between consecutive zeros satisfies the exact rational identity \begin{equation}\label{eq:rho-exact} \rho(a_k, a_{k+1}) = \frac{a_{k+1} - a_k}{1 - a_k a_{k+1}} = \frac{2k + 1}{2k^2 + 2k - 1} < \frac{1}{k - 1} \longrightarrow 0 \qquad (k \to \infty). \end{equation} Consequently, $\{a_k\}$ is neither uniformly Frostman nor separated in the pseudohyperbolic metric.

3.The Universal Pole-Free Exponential Decay Trick

Let $B(z) = \prod_{k=1}^\infty b_k(z)$, where $b_k(z) = \frac{a_k - z}{1 - a_k z}$ and $a_k \in [0, 1)$. Differentiating $B(z)$ via the product rule and taking moduli gives \begin{equation}\label{eq:Bprime-sum} B'(z) = \sum_{k=1}^\infty b_k'(z) B_k(z) = \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{a_k^2 - 1}{(1 - a_k z)^2} B_k(z), \qquad |B'(z)| \le \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{1 - a_k^2}{|1 - a_k z|^2} |B_k(z)|, \end{equation} where $B_k(z) = \prod_{j \ne k} b_j(z)$ is the subproduct with the $k$-th factor omitted.
