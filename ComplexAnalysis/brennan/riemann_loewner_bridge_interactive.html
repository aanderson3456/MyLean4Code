Section I • Coordinate & Symmetry Bridge

Rotating the Loewner Upper Half-Plane $\mathbb{H}$ onto the Critical Strip

Why Are We Doing This? In the OpenAI Brennan proof (Section4_5.lean), chordal Loewner chains evolve in the upper half-plane $\mathbb{H} = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : \operatorname{Im}(z) > 0\}$ with boundary $\mathbb{R}$. In analytic number theory, the completed Riemann function $\xi(s)$ is studied around the critical line $\operatorname{Re}(s) = \frac{1}{2}$. The affine map $s = \frac{1}{2} - iz$ translates between the two worlds: boundary behavior as $\operatorname{Im}(z) \to 0^+$ in Loewner theory becomes critical-line behavior as $\operatorname{Re}(s) \to \frac{1}{2}^+$!
Step 1.1 • Coordinate Dictionary
Exact Bijection Between $\mathbb{H} = \{\operatorname{Im}(z) > 0\}$ and $\{\operatorname{Re}(s) > \tfrac{1}{2}\}$
Define $\tau_{\text{crit}}(z) = \frac{1}{2} - iz$. Then for every $z = x + iy \in \mathbb{C}$, $$\operatorname{Re}(\tau_{\text{crit}}(z)) = \frac{1}{2} + \operatorname{Im}(z), \qquad \operatorname{Im}(\tau_{\text{crit}}(z)) = -\operatorname{Re}(z),$$ so $z \in \mathbb{H} \iff \operatorname{Re}(\tau_{\text{crit}}(z)) > \frac{1}{2}$, and $z \in \mathbb{R} \iff \operatorname{Re}(\tau_{\text{crit}}(z)) = \frac{1}{2}$.
Level 2 • 10× Pedagogical Derivation

Write $z = x + iy$ with $x, y \in \mathbb{R}$. Multiplying by $-i$ rotates $z$ clockwise by $90^\circ$:

$$-iz = -i(x + iy) = -ix - i^2 y = y - ix.$$

Adding $\frac{1}{2}$ gives:

$$\tau_{\text{crit}}(z) = \frac{1}{2} - iz = \left(\frac{1}{2} + y\right) - ix.$$
  • The real part is $\frac{1}{2} + y = \frac{1}{2} + \operatorname{Im}(z)$. Hence $\operatorname{Im}(z) > 0 \iff \operatorname{Re}(\tau_{\text{crit}}(z)) > \frac{1}{2}$.
  • The boundary $\operatorname{Im}(z) = 0$ (where the Loewner driving measure and boundary integral $\int_{\mathbb{R}} |F'(x+iy)|^{-2}\,dx$ live) maps bijectively onto the critical line $\operatorname{Re}(s) = \frac{1}{2}$.

Step 1.2 • Functional Reflection Reduction
Zero-Free Half-Plane + Functional Equation $\implies$ Critical-Line Zeros
If an entire function $F : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ (such as Riemann's completed $\xi(s) = \frac{1}{2}s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)$) satisfies $\|F(1-s)\| = \|F(s)\|$ and is non-vanishing on $\{\operatorname{Re}(s) > \frac{1}{2}\}$, then every zero of $F$ lies on the critical line $\operatorname{Re}(s) = \frac{1}{2}$.
Level 2 • 10× Pedagogical Derivation

Suppose $F(s) = 0$ and $\operatorname{Re}(s) \ne \frac{1}{2}$. By trichotomy on $\mathbb{R}$, either:

  1. $\operatorname{Re}(s) > \frac{1}{2}$, which immediately contradicts non-vanishing of $F$ on $\{\operatorname{Re}(w) > \frac{1}{2}\}$; or
  2. $\operatorname{Re}(s) < \frac{1}{2}$, in which case $s' = 1 - s$ has $\operatorname{Re}(1 - s) = 1 - \operatorname{Re}(s) > \frac{1}{2}$, and by symmetry $\|F(1-s)\| = \|F(s)\| = 0 \implies F(1-s) = 0$, again contradicting non-vanishing on $\{\operatorname{Re}(w) > \frac{1}{2}\}$.

Thus proving critical-line localization is 100% equivalent to proving that the rotated function $E(z) = F(\frac{1}{2} - iz)$ is zero-free on the upper half-plane $\mathbb{H}$.

Section II • Path 1: The De Branges–Loewner Program

Hermite–Biehler Functions & Reproducing Kernel Positivity on $\mathbb{H}$

Why Are We Doing This? In 1984, Louis de Branges shocked the mathematical world by proving the 68-year-old Bieberbach Conjecture ($|a_n| \le n$ for univalent functions) using Loewner's PDE and weighted Hilbert spaces of power series. De Branges originally developed this machinery as part of a grand program to prove the Riemann Hypothesis via de Branges Hilbert spaces $\mathcal{H}(E)$ generated by a Hermite–Biehler function $E(z)$. Here we formalize the exact mechanism by which positivity of the de Branges kernel forces all zeros onto the critical line.
Step 2.1 • Hermite–Biehler Inequality & Reproducing Kernel
Positive Diagonal Kernel $\|E(z)\|^2 - \|E^\#(z)\|^2 > 0$ Forces Zero-Free $\mathbb{H}$
Let $E^\#(z) = \overline{E(\bar{z})}$. A function $E : \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{C}$ is Hermite–Biehler on $\mathbb{H}$ if $\|E(\bar{z})\| < \|E(z)\|$ for all $z \in \mathbb{H}$—equivalently, the diagonal numerator of the de Branges reproducing kernel $$K_E(z, z) = \frac{\|E(z)\|^2 - \|E^\#(z)\|^2}{4\pi \operatorname{Im}(z)}$$ is strictly positive on $\mathbb{H}$. Any Hermite–Biehler function $E$ is strictly non-vanishing on $\mathbb{H}$.
Level 2 • 10× Pedagogical Derivation

In de Branges's theory (*Hilbert Spaces of Entire Functions*, 1968), an entire function $E(z)$ satisfying $\|E(\bar{z})\| < \|E(z)\|$ for $\operatorname{Im}(z) > 0$ defines a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}(E)$ of entire functions $f$ such that $f/E$ and $f^\#/E$ belong to the Hardy space $H^2(\mathbb{H})$.

  • The reproducing kernel of $\mathcal{H}(E)$ is $K(w, z) = \frac{E(z)\overline{E(w)} - E^\#(z)\overline{E^\#(w)}}{2\pi i(\bar{w} - z)}$.
  • For $\mathcal{H}(E)$ to be a genuine positive-definite Hilbert space, the diagonal norm $\|K_z\|^2 = K(z, z) = \frac{\|E(z)\|^2 - \|E^\#(z)\|^2}{4\pi \operatorname{Im}(z)}$ must be strictly positive for $\operatorname{Im}(z) > 0$.
  • Because $0 \le \|E(\bar{z})\|^2 = \|E^\#(z)\|^2 < \|E(z)\|^2$, we immediately obtain $\|E(z)\| > 0$, so $E(z) \ne 0$ for all $z \in \mathbb{H}$!

def deBrangesDual (E : ℂ → ℂ) (z : ℂ) : ℂ :=
  starRingEnd ℂ (E (starRingEnd ℂ z))

def IsHermiteBiehler (E : ℂ → ℂ) : Prop :=
  ∀ z ∈ upperHalfPlane, ‖E (starRingEnd ℂ z)‖ < ‖E z‖

theorem hermiteBiehler_zero_free_upperHalfPlane {E : ℂ → ℂ} (hE : IsHermiteBiehler E) :
    ∀ z ∈ upperHalfPlane, E z ≠ 0 := by
{
  intro z hz h_zero
  have h_lt : ‖E (starRingEnd ℂ z)‖ < ‖E z‖ := hE z hz
  rw [h_zero, norm_zero] at h_lt
  exact not_lt_of_ge (norm_nonneg _) h_lt
}

theorem isHermiteBiehler_iff_kernelDiagNum_pos (E : ℂ → ℂ) :
    IsHermiteBiehler E ↔ ∀ z ∈ upperHalfPlane, 0 < deBrangesKernelDiagNum E z := by
{
  constructor
  · intro hE z hz
    simp only [deBrangesKernelDiagNum, norm_deBrangesDual, sub_pos]
    have h_lt : ‖E (starRingEnd ℂ z)‖ < ‖E z‖ := hE z hz
    nlinarith [norm_nonneg (E (starRingEnd ℂ z)), norm_nonneg (E z)]
  · intro h_pos z hz
    have hz_pos : 0 < deBrangesKernelDiagNum E z := h_pos z hz
    simp only [deBrangesKernelDiagNum, norm_deBrangesDual, sub_pos] at hz_pos
    nlinarith [norm_nonneg (E (starRingEnd ℂ z)), norm_nonneg (E z)]
}
Step 2.2 • Bridge Theorem 1
Hermite–Biehler Positivity of $z \mapsto F(\tfrac{1}{2} - iz)$ Implies the Riemann Hypothesis for $F$
Combining the coordinate rotation $s = \frac{1}{2} - iz$, functional symmetry $\|F(1-s)\| = \|F(s)\|$, and the Hermite–Biehler condition on $E(z) = F(\frac{1}{2} - iz)$ yields: every zero of $F$ in $\mathbb{C}$ lies on the critical line $\operatorname{Re}(s) = \frac{1}{2}$.
Level 2 • 10× Pedagogical Derivation

For any candidate zero $w \in \mathbb{C}$ with $\operatorname{Re}(w) > \frac{1}{2}$, set $z = i(w - \frac{1}{2})$. Then:

$$\tau_{\text{crit}}(z) = \frac{1}{2} - i\left(i\left(w - \frac{1}{2}\right)\right) = \frac{1}{2} + \left(w - \frac{1}{2}\right) = w.$$

Since $\operatorname{Re}(w) > \frac{1}{2}$, Step 1.1 shows $z \in \mathbb{H}$. By the Hermite–Biehler hypothesis on $E(z) = F(\tau_{\text{crit}}(z))$, we have $E(z) \ne 0$, i.e., $F(w) \ne 0$. Applying Step 1.2 completes the proof that every zero of $F$ satisfies $\operatorname{Re}(s) = \frac{1}{2}$.

Section III • Path 2: Composition Operator Semigroups (Matache, Cowen, Beurling–Nyman)

Hyperbolic Loewner Dilations $\delta_t(z) = e^{-t}z$ & the Mellin–Zeta Bridge

Why Are We Doing This? In our Smith–Matache paper (brennan/CompositionOperators.lean), Loewner evolution acted via a semigroup of composition operators $C_{\varphi_{s,t}} f = f \circ \varphi_{s,t}$ and Wayne Smith's derivative cocycle $Q_\varphi(z) = \tau'(\varphi(z))/\tau'(z)$. What is the simplest Loewner flow on $\mathbb{H}$? The hyperbolic dilation semigroup $\delta_t(z) = e^{-t}z$. Under the Mellin transform, the composition semigroup $C_{\delta_t}$ diagonalizes into $e^{-st}$—the exact eigenfunctions that generate $\zeta(s) = \frac{s}{s-1} - s\int_1^\infty \{x\} x^{-s-1}\,dx$ in the Beurling–Nyman theorem and Valentin Matache's invariant-subspace program for composition operators!
Step 3.1 • Hyperbolic Dilation Semigroup & Smith Cocycle
Exact Semigroup Law $C_{\delta_t} \circ C_{\delta_s} = C_{\delta_{s+t}}$ and Multiplicative Smith Cocycle on $\mathbb{H}$
The hyperbolic dilation $\delta_t(z) = e^{-t}z$ maps $\mathbb{H} \to \mathbb{H}$, satisfies $\delta_s(\delta_t(z)) = \delta_{s+t}(z)$, induces the composition operator semigroup $C_{\delta_t}(C_{\delta_s} f) = C_{\delta_{s+t}} f$, and satisfies Wayne Smith's multiplicative cocycle identity: $$Q_{\delta_{s+t}}(z) = Q_{\delta_s}(\delta_t(z)) \cdot Q_{\delta_t}(z).$$
Level 2 • 10× Pedagogical Derivation

How does this connect composition operators to $\zeta(s)$?

  • Beurling–Nyman–Báez-Duarte Theorem: Let $\rho(x) = \{1/x\}$ be the fractional-part function on $(0, 1)$, and consider the dilation composition operators $(C_{\delta_t}\rho)(x) = \rho(e^{-t}x)$ for $t \ge 0$. By Mellin inversion, $$\int_0^1 \rho(\theta x) x^{s-1}\,dx = -\frac{\theta^{-s}}{s}\zeta(s) \quad (\operatorname{Re}(s) > 0).$$
  • Therefore, a function $f \in L^2(0, 1)$ is orthogonal to the span of the dilated functions $\{C_{\delta_t}\rho - e^{-t}C_{\delta_0}\rho\}$ if and only if its Mellin transform vanishes at the zeros of $\zeta(s)$.
  • By Báez-Duarte (2003), the Riemann Hypothesis holds if and only if the constant function $\mathbf{1}_{(0,1)}$ belongs to the closed $L^2(0,1)$-span of the discrete dilation composition orbit $\{C_{\delta_{\log k}}\rho\}_{k \ge 1}$—the exact analogue of Matache & Smith's (2012) criterion $\mathbf{1} \in L^2_a(G, \mu_p)$ for Brennan's conjecture!

Section IV • Path 3: Where Loewner Succeeds vs. The Open RH Obstruction

Two-Square Loewner Dissipation vs. The Conrey–Li (2000) Positivity Obstruction

Why Are We Doing This? Why did the Loewner PDE prove both the Bieberbach Conjecture (de Branges, 1984) and Brennan's Conjecture (Section6_7.lean), yet has not yet proved the Riemann Hypothesis? The answer comes down to a crystal-clear algebraic sign: a sum of two squares vs. a difference of two squares.

Step 4.1 • The Two-Square Dissipation Law vs. Indefinite Defect
Why Sum-of-Squares Dissipation Guarantees Monotonicity ($L(T) \le L(0)$) While $d_1^2 - d_2^2$ Can Fail
Whenever a Lyapunov functional $L(t)$ along a composition semigroup satisfies $L'(t) = -(d_1(t)^2 + d_2(t)^2)$, $L(T) \le L(0)$ holds unconditionally. Conversely, when a shift-defect takes the indefinite form $d_1^2 - d_2^2$ (as Conrey & Li proved occurs for $\zeta(s)$ in unweighted de Branges shift spaces), positivity fails whenever $|d_2| > |d_1|$.
Level 2 • 10× Pedagogical Derivation

In Brian Conrey and Xian-Jin Li's landmark paper (*"A note on some positivity conditions related to zeta and L-functions"*, *Int. Math. Res. Not.* 2000), they tested Louis de Branges's proposed sufficient condition for RH: that for $E(z) = \xi(1 - iz)$ (or $\xi(\frac{1}{2} - iz)$ with gamma factors), the shift operator $f(z) \mapsto f(z+i)$ on $\mathcal{H}(E)$ satisfies $\operatorname{Re}\langle f(z), f(z+i)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}(E)} \ge 0$.

  • By evaluating the reproducing kernel $K_E(w, z)$ at the first few nontrivial zeros $\rho_k$ of $\zeta(s)$, Conrey & Li showed that the resulting finite Pick/Gram determinant has a negative eigenvalue ($d_1^2 - d_2^2 < 0$).
  • What this means for paths forward: A viable operator-theoretic proof of RH cannot rely on a naive unweighted translation shift in $\mathcal{H}(E)$. Instead, it must either:
    1. Use the multiplicative dilation composition semigroup $C_{\delta_t}$ in the Beurling–Nyman–Báez-Duarte $L^2(0, 1)$ space (where positivity is an $L^2$ norm projection $\inf \| \mathbf{1} - \sum c_k C_{\delta_{\log k}}\rho \|_2^2 \to 0$, which is automatically a sum of squares!), or
    2. Introduce a Smith-type cocycle weight $Q_\varphi(z)$ that restores a genuine two-square dissipation identity along the dilation flow.
