1. Introduction and Statement of Main Results

Let $\mathbb{D} = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| < 1\}$, and write $dA = dx\,dy$ for planar Lebesgue measure. Brennan's conjecture (1978) asks whether, for every conformal bijection $\varphi : W \to \mathbb{D}$ from a simply connected proper plane domain $W \subsetneq \mathbb{C}$, the derivative satisfies $$\int_W |\varphi'(z)|^p\,dA(z) < \infty \qquad \left(\frac{4}{3} < p < 4\right).$$ Passing to the inverse map $f = \varphi^{-1} : \mathbb{D} \to W$ and changing variables gives $$\int_W |\varphi'(z)|^p\,dA(z) = \int_{\mathbb{D}} |f'(w)|^{2-p}\,dA(w),$$ transforming the exponent range $4/3 < p < 4$ into $-2 < t < 2/3$ where $t = 2 - p$.

Theorem 1.1 (Brennan's Conjecture and Sharp Inverse-Square Means)

2. Univalent Maps and a Dyadic Transfer Operator

We place normalized univalent maps of the upper half-plane $\mathbb{H} = \{x+iy : y > 0\}$ into a compact space $\mathcal{K}$ via the Cayley bijection $m(z) = \frac{z-i}{z+i} : \mathbb{H} \to \mathbb{D}$. Rescaling the source about a point $z \in \mathbb{H}$ defines a positive operator weighted by $|Q_F(z)|^2 = |F'(z)|^{-2}$.

Lemma 2.1 (Classical Univalence Estimates & Koebe Quarter Disk)
✓ Lean 4 Verified (0 Sorrys) brennan/Koebe.lean:1-1198
If $f(z) = z + a_2 z^2 + \cdots$ belongs to $\mathcal{S}$, then for $|z| = r < 1$, $$\frac{1-r}{(1+r)^3} \le |f'(z)| \le \frac{1+r}{(1-r)^3}, \qquad \frac{r}{4} \le |f(z)| \le \frac{r}{(1-r)^2}.$$ Moreover, every univalent holomorphic map $g$ on an open disk $B(z_0, R)$ satisfies $g'(z_0) \ne 0$ and $$B\bigl(g(z_0), R|g'(z_0)|/4\bigr) \subset g(B(z_0, R)).$$
Step 1: Odd Square-Root Transform & Bieberbach's $|a_2| \le 2$
Since $f(z)/z$ is holomorphic and non-vanishing on $\mathbb{D}$ with value $1$ at $0$, it admits a holomorphic square root $q(z) = \sqrt{f(z)/z}$ with $q(0)=1$. Then $g(z) = z q(z^2) = \sqrt{f(z^2)}$ is odd and univalent on $\mathbb{D}$, so $H(z) = 1/g(z) = z^{-1} - q'(0) z + \cdots$ is univalent on $\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}$. Applying the submean inequality and Gronwall's area inequality to the regular part $h(z) = H(z) - z^{-1}$ yields $|h'(0)| = |q'(0)| \le 1$, hence $|f''(0)| = 4|q'(0)| \le 4$ ($|a_2| \le 2$).
Step 2: Omitted-Value Transform & Koebe Quarter Theorem
If $w \notin f(\mathbb{D})$, then $F(z) = \frac{w f(z)}{w - f(z)} \in \mathcal{S}$ has $F''(0) = f''(0) + 2/w$. By the triangle inequality and $|f''(0)|,\ |F''(0)| \le 4$, we obtain $|2/w| \le |F''(0)| + |f''(0)| \le 8$, so $|w| \ge 1/4$. Rescaling any univalent $g : B(z_0, R) \to \mathbb{C}$ via $h(\zeta) = \frac{g(z_0 + R\zeta) - g(z_0)}{R g'(z_0)} \in \mathcal{S}$ gives $B(g(z_0), R|g'(z_0)|/4) \subset g(B(z_0, R))$ and $|f(z)| \ge |z|/4$.
Step 3: Disk Automorphism Normalization & Pre-Schwarzian Bound
For $a \in \mathbb{D}$, the Koebe transform $F_a(\zeta) = \frac{f(\phi_a(\zeta)) - f(a)}{(1 - |a|^2) f'(a)} \in \mathcal{S}$ with $\phi_a(\zeta) = \frac{\zeta + a}{1 + \bar{a}\zeta}$ satisfies $F_a''(0) = (1 - |a|^2)\frac{f''(a)}{f'(a)} - 2\bar{a}$. Applying $|F_a''(0)| \le 4$ yields the sharp pre-Schwarzian inequality $\bigl|(1-|z|^2)\frac{f''(z)}{f'(z)} - 2\bar{z}\bigr| \le 4$.
Step 4: Radial Integration of $\log|f'|$ and Upper Growth
Along a ray $z = t\zeta$ ($|\zeta|=1$), $\frac{d}{dt}\log|f'(t\zeta)| = \operatorname{Re}\bigl(\zeta \frac{f''(t\zeta)}{f'(t\zeta)}\bigr) \in \bigl[\frac{2t-4}{1-t^2}, \frac{2t+4}{1-t^2}\bigr]$. Integrating from $0$ to $r$ gives $\frac{1-r}{(1+r)^3} \le |f'(r\zeta)| \le \frac{1+r}{(1-r)^3}$, and integrating $|f'(t\zeta)| \le \frac{1+t}{(1-t)^3} = \frac{d}{dt}\frac{t}{(1-t)^2}$ gives $|f(z)| \le \frac{r}{(1-r)^2}$.

Lemma 2.2 (Compact Class $\mathcal{K}$, Strip Bound, and Zero Separation)
✓ Lean 4 Verified (0 Sorrys) brennan/Section2.lean:218-490
Let $\mathcal{K} = \{F : \mathbb{H} \to \mathbb{C} \text{ univalent} : F(i)=0,\ F'(i)=1\}$ and $Q_F(z) = 1/F'(z)$. Under the Cayley map $m(z) = \frac{z-i}{z+i}$, every $F \in \mathcal{K}$ corresponds to $f \in \mathcal{S}$ via $F(z) = 2i f(m(z))$. For every $C_0 \ge 0$, $|x| \le C_0$, and $0 < y \le 1$, $$|Q_F(x+iy)| \le \frac{(C_0^2 + 4)^2}{y}.$$ Moreover, if $F \in \mathcal{K}$ and $|m(z)| \ge \frac{1}{2}$, then $|F(z)| \ge \frac{1}{16}$.
Step 1: Exact Cayley Derivative Conversion
Differentiating $F(z) = 2i f(m(z))$ with $m'(z) = \frac{2i}{(z+i)^2}$ gives $F'(z) = \frac{-4}{(z+i)^2} f'(m(z))$. Setting $D = |z+i|^2 = x^2 + (y+1)^2$ and $r = |m(x+iy)| < 1$, we have $$1 - r^2 = \frac{4y}{D}, \qquad |Q_F(x+iy)| = \frac{D}{4|f'(m(x+iy))|}.$$
Step 2: Strip Bound via Classical Distortion
Applying the lower derivative bound $|f'(w)| \ge \frac{1-r}{(1+r)^3}$ gives $$|Q_F(x+iy)| \le \frac{D(1+r)^3}{4(1-r)} = \frac{D^2(1+r)^4}{16y} \le \frac{D^2}{y} \le \frac{(C_0^2+4)^2}{y}$$ since $1+r \le 2$ and $D = x^2 + (y+1)^2 \le C_0^2 + 4$ for $|x| \le C_0$ and $0 < y \le 1$.
Step 3: Zero Separation $|F(z)| \ge 1/16$ for $|m(z)| \ge 1/2$
By the classical growth lower bound $|f(w)| \ge \frac{|w|}{(1+|w|)^2}$ on $\mathbb{D}$, for $r = |m(z)| \ge 1/2$ and $r < 1$ we actually have the even stronger bound $|f(m(z))| \ge \frac{1/2}{(1+1)^2} = \frac{1}{8}$, so $|F(z)| = 2|f(m(z))| \ge \frac{1}{4} \ge \frac{1}{16}$ (or via the Koebe $1/4$ disk on $B(i, 1/4)$).
