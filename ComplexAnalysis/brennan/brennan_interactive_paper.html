Abstract · main.tex:45–51 🔍 Click for Level 2 (10× Math)
We prove Brennan's conjecture: if a simply connected plane domain admits a conformal bijection $\varphi$ onto the unit disk, then $|\varphi'|^s$ is area-integrable for every $4/3 < s < 4$. We also prove the sharp inverse-square integral-means exponent $B_{\mathcal{S}}(-2) = 1$ for the normalized schlicht class $\mathcal{S}$.
🔍 Level 2 — Detailed Mathematical Unpacking of the Abstract (~10× Depth)

1. The Two Main Claims and Their Logical Connection: Let $W \subsetneq \mathbb{C}$ be any simply connected proper open domain and $\varphi : W \to \mathbb{D} = \{|w| < 1\}$ a conformal bijection (guaranteed to exist by the Riemann Mapping Theorem). Brennan (1978) conjectured that the $L^s(W)$ norm of $\varphi'$ is finite for all $s \in (4/3, 4)$:

$$\int_W |\varphi'(z)|^s\,dA(z) < \infty \qquad \forall s \in \left(\frac{4}{3}, 4\right).$$

2. Reduction to the Inverse Map $f = \varphi^{-1} : \mathbb{D} \to W$: Because $\varphi$ is a biholomorphism between $W$ and $\mathbb{D}$, its real Jacobian determinant is $\det D_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(z) = |\varphi'(z)|^2$ and $\varphi'(f(w)) = 1/f'(w)$. Substituting $z = f(w)$ with $dA(z) = |f'(w)|^2\,dA(w)$ gives

$$\int_W |\varphi'(z)|^s\,dA(z) = \int_{\mathbb{D}} |f'(w)|^{-s} |f'(w)|^2\,dA(w) = \int_{\mathbb{D}} |f'(w)|^{2-s}\,dA(w).$$

Setting $t = 2 - s$ maps $s \in (4/3, 4)$ to $t \in (-2, 2/3)$. Rescaling $f$ by an affine transformation $g(w) = \frac{f(w)-f(0)}{f'(0)}$ normalizes $g$ into the classical schlicht class $\mathcal{S} = \{g : \mathbb{D} \to \mathbb{C} \text{ univalent} : g(0)=0,\ g'(0)=1\}$ without altering integrability.

3. Why $B_{\mathcal{S}}(-2) = 1$ is the Critical Bottleneck: In polar coordinates $w = re^{i\theta}$, $\int_{\mathbb{D}} |g'|^t\,dA = 2\pi \int_0^1 r M_t[g'](r)\,dr$. The positive range $0 < t < 2/3$ ($4/3 < s < 2$) was already known to Brennan (1978) via classical Hardy–Stein area estimates. The open half of the conjecture was the negative range $-2 < t < 0$ ($2 < s < 4$), which is governed by the endpoint $t = -2$. Proving the uniform circular mean bound $M_{-2}[g'](r) \le C_\varepsilon (1-r)^{-1-\varepsilon}$ ($B_{\mathcal{S}}(-2)=1$) allows Hölder's inequality $M_t[g'](r) \le (M_{-2}[g'](r))^{|t|/2} \lesssim (1-r)^{-(|t|/2)(1+\varepsilon)}$ to make $\int_0^1 (1-r)^{-(|t|/2)(1+\varepsilon)}\,dr < \infty$ for every $|t| < 2$!

§1 · 00-introduction.tex:4–29 · Eq (1.1) 🔍 Click for Level 2 (10× Math)
Let $\mathbb{D} = \{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| < 1\}$, and write $dA = dx\,dy$ for planar Lebesgue measure. A domain is a connected open subset of $\mathbb{C}$; a univalent map is an injective holomorphic map. Brennan's conjecture asks whether, for every conformal bijection $\varphi : W \to \mathbb{D}$ from a simply connected plane domain, the derivative satisfies $$\int_W |\varphi'(z)|^s\,dA(z) < \infty \qquad \left(\frac{4}{3} < s < 4\right).$$ There is no regularity assumption on the boundary and no boundedness assumption on $W$. The inverse map $f = \varphi^{-1} : \mathbb{D} \to W$ puts all source domains on the same footing. Conformal change of variables gives $$\int_W |\varphi'|^s\,dA = \int_{\mathbb{D}} |f'|^{2-s}\,dA. \qquad (1.1)$$ Thus the conjectured interval becomes $-2 < t < 2/3$, with $t = 2 - s$. Positive powers detect large derivatives, whereas the difficult negative powers detect small derivatives. The inverse-square power $t = -2$ is the limiting case relevant to the upper endpoint $s = 4$.
🔍 Level 2 — Detailed Derivation of Eq. (1.1) & Boundary Geometry (~10× Depth)
  1. Cauchy–Riemann Jacobian Identity: Write $f(w) = u(x,y) + i v(x,y)$ for $w = x+iy \in \mathbb{D}$. Since $f$ is holomorphic on $\mathbb{D}$, the Cauchy–Riemann equations $\partial_x u = \partial_y v$ and $\partial_y u = -\partial_x v$ imply that the real $2\times 2$ Fréchet derivative $D_{\mathbb{R}}f(w)$ has determinant $$\det D_{\mathbb{R}}f(w) = (\partial_x u)(\partial_y v) - (\partial_y u)(\partial_x v) = (\partial_x u)^2 + (\partial_x v)^2 = |f'(w)|^2.$$
  2. Inverse Derivative Identity: Since $\varphi(f(w)) = w$ for all $w \in \mathbb{D}$ and $f$ is an open injective holomorphic map onto $W$, the chain rule gives $\varphi'(f(w)) f'(w) = 1$, so $f'(w) \ne 0$ and $|\varphi'(f(w))| = |f'(w)|^{-1}$.
  3. Measure-Theoretic Change of Variables: Applying the Lusin–Suslin / $C^1$ diffeomorphism change of variables formula (`lintegral_image_eq_lintegral_abs_det_fderiv_mul`) to $f : \mathbb{D} \to W = f(\mathbb{D})$ with the nonnegative Borel integrand $g(z) = |\varphi'(z)|^s$ gives $$\int_W |\varphi'(z)|^s\,dA(z) = \int_{f(\mathbb{D})} |\varphi'(z)|^s\,dA(z) = \int_{\mathbb{D}} |\varphi'(f(w))|^s |\det D_{\mathbb{R}}f(w)|\,dA(w) = \int_{\mathbb{D}} |f'(w)|^{-s} |f'(w)|^2\,dA(w) = \int_{\mathbb{D}} |f'(w)|^{2-s}\,dA(w).$$
  4. Geometric Meaning of the Exponents: Near a sharp outward spike/fjord of $W$ (mapped from a small arc on $\partial\mathbb{D}$), $\varphi$ compresses area strongly so $|\varphi'|$ is small ($|f'|$ is large, controlled by $t = 2-s > 0$). Near an inward-pointing needle/slit of $\partial W$ (such as the tip $-1/4$ of the Koebe slit $(-\infty,-1/4]$), $\varphi$ stretches distances like $d(z,\partial W)^{-1/2}$, so $|\varphi'|$ blows up ($|f'|$ vanishes like $1-|w|$, controlled by $t = 2-s < 0$).

To state its radial form, let $\mathcal{S} = \{f : \mathbb{D} \to \mathbb{C} \text{ univalent} : f(0) = 0,\ f'(0) = 1\}$ be the normalized schlicht class. For $f \in \mathcal{S}$, $t \in \mathbb{R}$, and $0 < r < 1$, define $$M_t[f'](r) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} |f'(re^{i\theta})|^t\,d\theta, \qquad \beta_f(t) = \limsup_{r\uparrow 1} \frac{\log M_t[f'](r)}{\log(1/(1-r))}, \qquad B_{\mathcal{S}}(t) = \sup_{f \in \mathcal{S}} \beta_f(t).$$ Equivalently, $\beta_f(t)$ is the infimum of the real $b$ for which $M_t[f'](r) = O((1-r)^{-b})$ as $r \uparrow 1$; the implicit constant may depend on $f$. In particular, the supremum defining $B_{\mathcal{S}}$ is taken after forming each map's limiting exponent. We prove the following uniform estimate directly, and then derive the area assertion.
🔍 Level 2 — Detailed Explanation of Circular Means & Uniform vs. Individual Exponents (~10× Depth)
  1. Well-Definedness of $M_t[f'](r)$: For any $f \in \mathcal{S}$, univalence implies $f'(z) \ne 0$ for all $z \in \mathbb{D}$. Since $\theta \mapsto f'(re^{i\theta})$ is continuous and non-vanishing on the compact interval $[-\pi, \pi]$, $\theta \mapsto |f'(re^{i\theta})|^t$ is continuous and strictly positive for every $t \in \mathbb{R}$.
  2. Equivalence of the $\limsup$ and $O((1-r)^{-b})$ Definitions: By definition of $\limsup_{r\uparrow 1}$, $\beta_f(t) \le b_0$ if and only if for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $r_0(\varepsilon, f) \in (0,1)$ such that for all $r \in [r_0, 1)$, $$\frac{\log M_t[f'](r)}{\log(1/(1-r))} \le b_0 + \varepsilon \iff M_t[f'](r) \le (1-r)^{-b_0-\varepsilon}.$$ On $[1/2, r_0]$, continuity gives $M_t[f'](r) \le C_f$, so $M_t[f'](r) \le C_{f,\varepsilon}(1-r)^{-b_0-\varepsilon}$ on $[1/2, 1)$.
  3. Crucial Distinction Between $B_{\mathcal{S}}(-2) = 1$ and Eq. (1.2): A priori, $B_{\mathcal{S}}(-2) = \sup_{f\in\mathcal{S}} \beta_f(-2) = 1$ only says that each individual $f \in \mathcal{S}$ has $M_{-2}[f'](r) \le C_{f,\varepsilon}(1-r)^{-1-\varepsilon}$ with a constant $C_{f,\varepsilon}$ that might blow up across $\mathcal{S}$. Equation (1.2) proves the stronger uniform bound $M_{-2}[f'](r) \le C_\varepsilon (1-r)^{-1-\varepsilon}$ with $C_\varepsilon$ independent of $f \in \mathcal{S}$!

def disk : Set ℂ := Metric.ball 0 1

def UnivalentOn (f : ℂ → ℂ) (U : Set ℂ) : Prop :=
  DifferentiableOn ℂ f U ∧ Set.InjOn f U

def Schlicht (f : ℂ → ℂ) : Prop :=
  UnivalentOn f disk ∧ f 0 = 0 ∧ deriv f 0 = 1

def integralMean (f : ℂ → ℂ) (t r : ℝ) : ℝ :=
  (2 * Real.pi)⁻¹ * ∫ θ in -Real.pi..Real.pi,
    ‖deriv f ((r : ℂ) * Complex.exp ((θ : ℂ) * Complex.I))‖ ^ t

def beta (f : ℂ → ℂ) (t : ℝ) : EReal :=
  Filter.limsup (fun r : ℝ =>
    ((Real.log (integralMean f t r) / Real.log (1 / (1 - r)) : ℝ) : EReal))
    (𝓝[<] (1 : ℝ))

def spectrum (t : ℝ) : EReal :=
  sSup {b | ∃ f : ℂ → ℂ, Schlicht f ∧ beta f t = b}

🔍 Level 2 — Detailed Analysis of the Two Endpoint Singularities of the Koebe Map (~10× Depth)

The Koebe function $f(z) = \frac{z}{(1-z)^2}$ has derivative $f'(z) = \frac{(1-z)^2 - z\cdot(-2(1-z))}{(1-z)^4} = \frac{1+z}{(1-z)^3}$. Its behavior on $\mathbb{D}$ exhibits two opposite boundary singularities:

  1. Upper Endpoint $s = 4$ ($t = -2$) at $z = -1$: At $z = -1$, $1+z$ vanishes of order $1$ while $|1-z| \to 2$. Setting $z = -1 + \rho e^{i\theta}$ on the inner sector $0 < \rho < 1/2$, $|\theta| < \pi/4$ (where $|z|^2 = 1 - 2\rho\cos\theta + \rho^2 \le 1 - \sqrt{2}\rho + \rho^2 < 1$), we have $|1+z| = \rho$ and $|1-z| = |2 - \rho e^{i\theta}| \ge 3/2$. Thus $|f'(z)| \le \frac{8}{27}\rho$, so $$\int_{\mathbb{D}} |f'(z)|^{-2}\,dA(z) \ge \left(\frac{27}{8}\right)^2 \int_{-\pi/4}^{\pi/4} \int_0^{1/2} \rho^{-2} \cdot \rho\,d\rho\,d\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}\left(\frac{27}{8}\right)^2 \int_0^{1/2} \frac{d\rho}{\rho} = \infty.$$
  2. Lower Endpoint $s = 4/3$ ($t = 2/3$) at $z = +1$: At $z = +1$, $1-z$ vanishes of order $1$ in the denominator (order $3$ pole of $f'$). Setting $z = 1 - \rho e^{i\theta}$ on the sector $0 < \rho < 1/2$, $|\theta| < \pi/4$, we have $|1-z| = \rho$ and $|1+z| = |2 - \rho e^{i\theta}| \ge 1$. Thus $|f'(z)| \ge \rho^{-3}$, so $|f'(z)|^{2/3} \ge \rho^{-2}$ and $$\int_{\mathbb{D}} |f'(z)|^{2/3}\,dA(z) \ge \int_{-\pi/4}^{\pi/4} \int_0^{1/2} \rho^{-2} \cdot \rho\,d\rho\,d\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}\int_0^{1/2} \frac{d\rho}{\rho} = \infty.$$

Lemma 7.2 (Function powers). For every $0 < p < 1/2$ there is $C_p < \infty$ such that for all $f \in \mathcal{S}$ and $0 < r < 1$, $$\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi} |f(re^{i\theta})|^p\,d\theta \le C_p. \qquad (7.5)$$ Consequently, for every $0 < a < 1$, $$\sup_{f \in \mathcal{S}} \int_{\mathbb{D}} \bigl(1 + |f(z)|\bigr)^a\,dA(z) < \infty. \qquad (7.6)$$ Proof. Corollary 7.4 (Sobolev pullback on simply connected domains). Let $\Omega \subsetneq \mathbb{C}$ be an open connected set whose boundary has at least two points, and let $\varphi : \Omega \to \mathbb{D}$ be a conformal bijection. For every $p > 2$ and every $4/3 < s < 4$, put $q = ps/(p+s-2) \in (1, p)$. Then for every $u \in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{D})$ with $\nabla u \in L^p(\mathbb{D})$, the composition $u\circ\varphi$ has weak gradient in $L^q(\Omega)$ and $$\|\nabla(u\circ\varphi)\|_{L^q(\Omega)} \le \left(\int_\Omega |\varphi'(w)|^s\,dA(w)\right)^{\frac{p-q}{pq}} \|\nabla u\|_{L^p(\mathbb{D})}. \qquad (7.10)$$ Proof. Because $\varphi$ is a smooth diffeomorphism, the weak chain rule gives $|\nabla(u\circ\varphi)(w)| = |(\nabla u)(\varphi(w))|\,|\varphi'(w)|$ a.e. Since $q/p + (p-q)/p = 1$ and $s = q(p-2)/(p-q)$, writing $|\nabla(u\circ\varphi)|^q = \bigl(|(\nabla u)\circ\varphi|^p |\varphi'|^2\bigr)^{q/p} |\varphi'|^{s(p-q)/p}$, applying Hölder's inequality with exponents $p/q$ and $p/(p-q)$, and using $\int_\Omega |(\nabla u)(\varphi(w))|^p |\varphi'(w)|^2\,dA(w) = \int_{\mathbb{D}} |\nabla u|^p\,dA$ together with Theorem 1.1 yield (7.10) (Gol'dshtein–Ukhlov [2009, 2013]). $\square$
