Abstract. In OpenAI's 199-page resolution of the Quasi-Riemann Hypothesis [The Quasi-Riemann Hypothesis: A Zero-Free Half-Plane $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 7/8$, Sept. 30, 2026], the nonvanishing of all finite-order Hecke $L$-functions over $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$ and all Dirichlet $L$-functions on $\Omega_{7/8} = \{s \in \mathbb{C} : \operatorname{Re}(s) > 7/8\}$ hinges on a striking rational inequality at the climax of Section 13 (Lemma 13.3): the worst-case compensated high-side Poisson row exponent $E_*$ satisfies $-E_* \ge \frac{49}{440640} > 10^{-4}$. In this paper, we trace the complete number-theoretic origin of every prime factor in $\frac{49}{440640}$ from Kubota–Patterson cubic theta reflection, two-step Poisson moment recursions over the Eisenstein integers $\mathcal{O} = \mathbb{Z}[\omega]$, and minimax witness balancing. Furthermore, we prove an exact Asymmetry Cancellation Law showing that the scale asymmetry $b = l_y - l_x$ cancels identically from the continuation balance equation, reducing the zero-free threshold to a piecewise-linear V-curve $\sigma_{\mathrm{II}}(\ell) = \frac{11}{12} - \frac{\ell}{4} + \frac{1}{2}\max(0, \ell - \frac{1}{6})$ with unique global minimum $\frac{7}{8}$ at $\ell = \frac{1}{6}$. Because the high side retains strict slack $\frac{49}{440640} > 0$, the $\frac{7}{8}$ barrier is held in place purely by two low-side trivial-bound exponents, and we give the exact linear criterion required to push the zero-free half-plane to $\frac{7}{8} - \varepsilon$ and $\frac{6}{7}$.

1.Introduction & Sarason _eps Common-Signal Continuation

Let $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}) = \mathbb{Q}(\omega)$ ($\omega^2 + \omega + 1 = 0$) with ring of integers $\mathcal{O} = \mathbb{Z}[\omega]$, and let $\Omega_{\sigma_0} = \{s \in \mathbb{C} : \operatorname{Re}(s) > \sigma_0\}$ denote the open right half-plane above $\sigma_0 \in \mathbb{R}$. By quadratic norm factorization, composing a Dirichlet character $\chi \bmod q$ with the ideal norm $N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}$ produces a finite-order Hecke character over $F$ whose $L$-function factors (up to finitely many nonvanishing Euler factors in $\Omega_0$) as $$L_F(s, \chi \circ N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}) = L(s, \chi)\,L(s, \chi \chi_{-3}).$$ Consequently, any zero-free half-plane $\Omega_{\sigma_0}$ for primitive finite-order Hecke $L$-functions $L_F(s, \eta)$ over $F$ transfers immediately to all Dirichlet $L$-functions $L(s, \chi)$ and to the Riemann zeta function $\zeta(s)$.

2.The Asymmetry Cancellation Law & the V-Curve $\sigma_{\mathrm{II}}(\ell)$

In Part II of [OAI26a, Section 5, Eq. Theorem 2.1 (Asymmetry Cancellation Law & Sharpness of the $7/8$ V-Curve). Under the scale relations \eqref{eq:scale-relations}, the principal Mellin shift is $C(s) = \frac{l_x}{2} + s - 1 + \frac{h}{6}$ and the low-side Cauchy–Schwarz probe exponent (combining Lemma 6.1 and Proposition 6.3 of [OAI26a]) is $$E_{\mathrm{low}}(\ell, b) = \frac{l_x}{2} + \frac{b}{12} + \frac{\max(0, \ell - 1/6)}{2}.$$ Equating $C(\sigma_0) = E_{\mathrm{low}}(\ell, b)$ cancels both $l_x/2$ and the asymmetry $b = l_y - l_x$ identically, yielding the universal V-curve \begin{equation}\label{eq:v-curve} \sigma_{\mathrm{II}}(\ell) = \frac{11}{12} - \frac{\ell}{4} + \frac{\max(0, \ell - 1/6)}{2} = \begin{cases} \dfrac{11}{12} - \dfrac{\ell}{4}, & \ell \le \dfrac{1}{6}, \\[8pt] \dfrac{19}{24} + \dfrac{\ell}{2}, & \ell \ge \dfrac{1}{6}. \end{cases} \end{equation} In particular, $\sigma_{\mathrm{II}}(0) = \frac{11}{12}$ (Stage I), $\sigma_{\mathrm{II}}(1/6) = \frac{7}{8}$ (Stage II), and $\sigma_{\mathrm{II}}(\ell) \ge \frac{7}{8}$ for all $\ell \in \mathbb{R}$ with equality if and only if $\ell = \frac{1}{6}$.

3.Two-Poisson Moment Capacities, $D_x$, $P_x$, and Minimax Witness Balancing

While the asymmetry $b = l_y - l_x$ cancels out of the low-side continuation threshold $\sigma_{\mathrm{II}}(\ell)$, it plays a decisive role on the High Side (Sections 10–13 of [OAI26a]): choosing $b = 1/8$ raises $h = \frac{1 + 3\ell + b}{2} = \frac{3}{4} + \frac{b}{2}$ from $\frac{3}{4}$ to $\frac{13}{16}$, giving the high-side moment estimates enough leverage to beat the nonprincipal Poisson rows.
