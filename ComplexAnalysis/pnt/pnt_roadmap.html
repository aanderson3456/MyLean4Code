PNT Formalization Roadmap

A multi-level translation from D.J. Newman's 1980 "Simple Analytic Proof of the Prime Number Theorem" down to the gritty, verified 50-line bounds of our Lean 4 formalization.

Step 1: The Tauberian Contour Integral

Level 1: The Paper (Math)

Newman's brilliant 1980 shortcut bounds the inverse Laplace integral over a bounded contour $W$ (the boundary of the truncated domain $\text{Re}(z) \ge -\delta, |z| \le R$). He multiplies the integrand by $(1 + z^2/R^2)$ to force the integral to converge neatly along the imaginary axis without needing infinite depth.

Level 2: The Formalization Strategy

Instead of relying on abstract Lebesgue measure theory to bind the integral limits, we isolate Newman's modifier into a standalone algebraic algebraic definition. This allows us to prove absolute bounds on the circle entirely within algebraic $\mathbb{C}$ arithmetic before ever touching an integral.

Level 3: The Gritty Lean 4

We defined the exact modifier and proved its exact geometric behavior on the boundary circle in ComplexAnalysis/Sarason/Contour.lean:

def newmanModifier (s : ℂ) (R : ℝ) : ℂ := (1 : ℂ) + s^2 / (R^2 : ℂ) lemma newmanModifier_eq_on_circle (s : ℂ) (R : ℝ) ... newmanModifier s R = (s + R^2 / s) / s

Step 2: Connecting Primes to the Zeta Function

Level 1: The Paper (Math)

To analyze the distribution of primes, we rely on Euler's fundamental identity bridging the Riemann Zeta function to the primes: $\zeta(s) = \prod_p (1 - p^{-s})^{-1}$. This implies $-\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)} = \sum_p \frac{\ln p}{p^s - 1}$.

Level 2: The Formalization Strategy

Infinite products are notoriously difficult to formalize constructively. Instead of chasing infinite bounds and generalized metric spaces, we bound everything into finite Finset structures and strict $N$-truncations. The infinite limit is replaced by classical epsilon-delta definitions on the partial Finset.prod.

Level 3: The Gritty Lean 4

We defined Weierstrass limit proxies in Definitions.lean and explicitly constructed the partial geometric bounds in Zeta.lean:

Step 3: Expanding the Prime Combinatorics

Level 1: The Paper (Math)

The paper implicitly relies on the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic to state that expanding the Euler product generates the sum over all integers $\sum n^{-s}$.

Level 2: The Formalization Strategy

Lean's compiler cannot "implicitly" expand an infinite product of infinite sums. We must manually bridge the Finsupp prime power mappings to the standard integers. We restrict this to a bounded set $S(N,K)$ (primes $\le N$, powers $\le K$) to avoid topology entirely.

Level 3: The Gritty Lean 4

We created a custom bijection verifying that the geometric combinatorial expansion precisely yields the finite integer sums in Zeta.lean:

lemma smooth_numbers_fta_bijection (N : ℕ) (n : ℕ) ... ∃! (f : ℕ →₀ ℕ), ... ∧ f.prod (fun p k => p ^ k) = n lemma euler_product_expand_sum (N K : ℕ) (s : ℂ) : ∏ p ∈ P, (∑ k, p^{-sk}) = ∑ n ∈ smoothNumbersBoundedFinset N K, n^{-s}

Step 4: The Chebyshev Target $\vartheta(x)$

Level 1: The Paper (Math)

Newman proves that $\vartheta(x) = \sum_{p \le x} \ln p \sim x$. He isolates $f(t) = \vartheta(e^t)e^{-t} - 1$ and proves its integral converges.
